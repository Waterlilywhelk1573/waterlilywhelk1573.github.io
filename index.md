## 🗂️ Organizing Your First Collection

A good first session takes about fifteen minutes and pays off for years.

**Create a clear folder structure.** Decide on a simple pattern such as Year, then Month, then Event. Point Photo Commander at the top-level folder so it can see everything beneath it.

**Run the duplicate scan.** Let the tool find repeated images. Review the results carefully and remove only the copies you are confident about.

**Rename in batches.** Use the batch rename feature to convert camera-generated names into readable ones. A pattern like "2026-05-Holiday-001" tells you far more than "IMG_4471".

**Apply tags.** Add keywords to the photos you are most likely to search for later — people's names, places, and occasions are the most useful.

**Rate your favorites.** Star ratings make it easy to filter down to the best shots when you want to share or print something.

## 🧰 Tips for Getting the Most Out of Photo Commander

Browse in full-screen mode when comparing similar shots; the larger view makes small differences in focus and exposure obvious.

Use the thumbnail size slider to switch between a quick overview of hundreds of images and a detailed look at a handful.

Keep your originals. When you use optimization or conversion tools, save the result as a new file unless you are certain you will never need the full-quality version again.

Back up before a big cleanup. Even careful users occasionally delete the wrong file, and a copy on an external drive costs very little.

Sort by capture date rather than file date when merging photos from a phone and a camera, because file dates often reflect when the image was copied rather than when it was taken.
